Healing with Attia is the website of Attia Altaf, a UK-qualified (CPCAB) integrative psychotherapist who works online, in Urdu, English and Punjabi, with clients in Pakistan, the UK, the UAE and Saudi Arabia. We designed and built it in one week, as a fast static site of 34 pages: one page for each condition she treats, pages for the cities and countries her clients live in, 13 articles so far, and booking through WhatsApp from every page.

## The brief

Attia works fully online, and her clients are spread across four countries. People looking for therapy are often anxious and unsure where to start, so the website had to feel calm, explain clearly what therapy involves, and make the first step easy.

It also had to be found. Someone in Lahore searching for anxiety treatment and someone in Dubai searching for an Urdu-speaking therapist are looking for the same person in different words, so the site needed a page for each of those searches.

## What we built

### Pages for what clients search for

- **Five condition pages:** depression, anxiety, OCD, trauma and postnatal depression, each explaining what therapy for it looks like
- **Seven location pages:** Lahore, Karachi, Islamabad, Dubai and the UAE, Saudi Arabia, the UK, and Pakistanis living abroad
- **13 articles**, written by us with Attia's help, that answer the questions people search before booking, from how to choose a therapist to whether online therapy works, the difference between a psychiatrist and a psychologist, and family pressures such as rishta stress and joint-family life
- A services page, an FAQ, an about page with her credentials, and a podcast page

### A calm design that builds trust

- An uncluttered layout and soft colours, with her credentials near the top of the page
- Plain explanations of each service, without clinical jargon
- A privacy policy and terms, which matter to people sharing something personal

### Booking where clients already are

- A WhatsApp button on every page, with the booking message already written
- No accounts or booking calendar to learn: a client messages, asks what they need to, and books. A contact form is there for anyone who prefers email.

## How it's built

The site is static: HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with no database or content system behind it. For a practice website that changes a few times a month, static pages load fast on any phone connection and leave nothing to update, patch or secure.

Search engines get help too. The home and service pages carry structured data that describes the practice as a professional service, lists the countries it serves and marks up its common questions, and every page has breadcrumbs.

## Results

- **1 week** from the start of the project to launch
- **34 pages**, each written for something her clients search for
- **More than 500 clients:** Attia has worked with over 500 clients so far (the practice's own figure, November 2026), and the website is where new clients find her and book

## Why it matters

For a service business, the website is often the first conversation. This one answers the questions a new client has before they ask, gives each search its own page, and turns that trust into a message on WhatsApp.
