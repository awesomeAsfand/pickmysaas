**The short answer:** start with one repetitive task that costs you time every week. Try an off-the-shelf AI tool on it for 30 days, with a person checking every result, and measure the time it saves before you pay for anything bigger. For most small businesses in the Gulf, the first wins are routine customer questions, writing in Arabic and English, and getting data out of documents.

You don't need a strategy document, a data team or a custom system to begin. You need one problem worth solving and a way to tell whether AI solved it.

## Where AI helps a small business first

These five jobs suit today's AI tools well: they're repetitive, they follow a pattern, and a person can check the result in seconds.

| Task | What AI does | What a person still does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Routine customer questions | Answers opening hours, prices, delivery areas and order status on your website or WhatsApp | Takes over complaints, refunds and anything unusual |
| Writing in Arabic and English | Drafts product descriptions, social posts, emails and replies in both languages | Checks facts, tone and dialect before anything is published |
| Reading documents | Pulls amounts, dates and names out of invoices, receipts, forms and CVs into a spreadsheet | Spot-checks the numbers, especially totals |
| Summarising | Turns meetings, long email threads and customer reviews into a few lines | Decides what to do about them |
| Sorting enquiries | Tags each new lead by service, budget and urgency | Calls the leads worth calling |

Notice the right-hand column. In each case AI does the first 80% of the work and a person does the part that needs judgement. That split is what makes AI safe to use in a small business.

## Where AI isn't ready to work alone

- **Facts and prices.** AI tools can state wrong information with total confidence. Never let one quote prices, stock or delivery dates unless it reads them from your own up-to-date data, and check it regularly.
- **Advice with consequences.** Legal, medical, financial and tax answers need a qualified person.
- **Decisions about people.** Don't let AI choose who to hire, who gets credit or which customer is turned away.
- **Your customers' dialect.** Arabic quality varies between tools and dialects. Test with real messages from your customers before you trust a tool to reply on its own.

## A 30-day plan to start

**Week 1: pick the task and measure it.** Choose one task from the table above. Write down how many hours a week it takes now and how often mistakes happen. Without this number you won't know whether AI helped.

**Week 2: set it up.** Try the task in one of the main assistants: ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude or Microsoft Copilot. Write clear instructions: who your customers are, the tone you use, what the AI must never say. Give it three or four good examples of past work. Most of the quality comes from these instructions, not from the tool you pick.

**Week 3: use it for real, with a check.** Run the real task through AI, and have a person review every result before it reaches a customer. Keep a simple log of each mistake and what caused it.

**Week 4: decide.** Compare the hours and mistakes with week 1. Then keep it, fix the instructions and run another month, or drop it and try the next task. A tool that saves two hours a week but needs an hour of checking is saving one hour, not two.

## An off-the-shelf tool or a custom build?

Most businesses should start with an off-the-shelf tool. A custom build only makes sense once you know the task is worth it.

| Start with an off-the-shelf tool when | Consider a custom build when |
| --- | --- |
| The task is general: writing, summarising, translating | The AI needs your own data: your catalogue, prices, bookings or policies |
| A few people use it a few times a day | Hundreds of customer conversations a day go through it |
| No customer personal data is involved | It has to work inside your website, WhatsApp or internal systems |
| You're still testing whether AI helps at all | It must hand over to a person cleanly, in Arabic and English |

A custom build usually means an assistant connected to your own information, so it answers from your real prices and policies instead of guessing, plus a clear handover to your team when it can't help.

## Rules to check before AI talks to your customers

**Customer data.** Saudi Arabia's Personal Data Protection Law and the UAE's Federal Decree-Law 45 of 2021 both set rules for sending personal data outside the country. Saudi rules call for a risk assessment before data is transferred abroad, as [King & Spalding's summary](https://www.kslaw.com/insights/articles/international-personal-data-transfers-under-saudi-arabias-data-protection-law) explains, and the UAE law allows transfers to countries with adequate protection or with the person's explicit consent, among other routes ([Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2021/11/uae-issues-landmark-personal-data-protection-law)). Most AI tools process data outside the Gulf. So keep customers' names, phone numbers and ID details out of free consumer tools, use business plans whose terms say your data isn't used for training, and ask a lawyer about your own case. We're not lawyers, and these rules change.

**WhatsApp.** From 15 January 2026, WhatsApp's Business Platform terms bar general-purpose AI assistants, the kind that will chat about anything. Bots that answer questions about your own business, support customers or help with sales are still allowed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/18/whatssapp-changes-its-terms-to-bar-general-purpose-chatbots-from-its-platform/)). So keep a WhatsApp bot focused on your business, and check Meta's current terms before you launch one.

**Honesty with customers.** Tell people when they're talking to an AI, and always give them a way to reach a person. It costs nothing and it protects the trust you've built.

## What it costs

The main AI assistants have a free tier and business plans priced per user per month; check each provider's current price page, because prices change often. The bigger cost is your time in weeks 2 and 3, writing instructions and checking results. A custom assistant costs more to build, plus a monthly bill that grows with how much it's used, so get one fixed quote for the build and an estimate of the monthly running cost before you agree to anything.
