**The short answer:** AI assistants recommend businesses they can find, understand and see confirmed on other sites. So let their crawlers read your website, put plain answers on your pages (what you sell, for whom, where, at what price), make sure other trusted sites describe your business the same way, and check every month what the assistants actually say. There's no trick or special file that does it for you.

More customers now ask ChatGPT, Gemini or Google's AI Overviews "who's the best clinic in Riyadh?" or "how much does a website cost in Dubai?" before they search the usual way. If the answer doesn't mention you, you never get the chance to compete.

## How AI assistants decide who to mention

- **Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode** draw on pages in Google's index. Google says a page is eligible to be shown as a supporting link if it's indexed and can appear in Search with a snippet, with [no additional technical requirements](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features).
- **ChatGPT search** finds pages with OpenAI's crawler, OAI-SearchBot. OpenAI says sites that block it [won't be shown in ChatGPT search answers](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots).
- **Every assistant** also repeats what other sites say about you: directories, review sites, news articles, partner pages. Your own website alone is rarely enough to be recommended.

That gives you four jobs.

## Step 1: Let them read your website

- **Check your robots.txt.** It shouldn't block Googlebot or OAI-SearchBot. OpenAI's training crawler, GPTBot, is a separate setting, so you can allow search without allowing training.
- **Check your hosting or firewall.** Some security settings block crawlers even when robots.txt allows them.
- **Check your pages are indexed.** In Google Search Console, inspect your main pages. A page Google hasn't indexed can't appear in its AI answers.
- **Check the AI setting in Search Console.** Google's [AI optimisation guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) says a site must be included in Google's generative AI features in Search Console settings.
- **Keep the important words in text.** Prices, services and opening hours locked inside images or PDFs are hard for any machine to read.

## Step 2: Answer customers' questions in plain words

- **Put the answer first.** The first paragraph of each service page should say what you offer, who it's for, where, how long it takes and what it costs. AI assistants quote clear, self-contained answers.
- **One page per main service.** A page that tries to cover everything answers nothing well.
- **Use real numbers.** "From SAR 1,125" or "ready in 4 to 8 weeks" is quotable. "Competitive prices" isn't.
- **Add a short FAQ** with the questions customers actually ask you on WhatsApp, in their words.
- **Write in Arabic and English** if your customers search in both. An assistant answering in Arabic looks for Arabic sources.

## Step 3: Be described the same way everywhere

- **Write one short description** of your business: name, what you do, where, for whom. Use the same facts on your website, social profiles and directory listings.
- **Add structured data.** Organization or LocalBusiness markup and FAQ markup state your facts in a format search engines read reliably. Keep it matching what's visible on the page.
- **Get listed where your customers look:** the main directories and review sites for your industry, and a Google Business Profile if your business is eligible.
- **Ask happy customers for reviews,** on the sites that matter in your industry. Never buy or write fake ones.
- **Earn mentions:** local press, industry associations, suppliers and partners who link to you.

## Step 4: Check what they actually say

Once a month, ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity the same ten questions your customers would ask, such as "best [your service] in [your city]" or "how much does [your service] cost in [your country]". Note whether you're mentioned, what's said about you, and which sites are cited. Those cited sites tell you where to get listed next.

In Google Search Console, clicks from AI Overviews are counted inside your normal web search totals, so there's no separate AI report. Watch overall impressions and clicks for the pages you've improved.

## What doesn't work

- **llms.txt files.** Google says llms.txt isn't needed for its AI features, and Google's John Mueller has said [no AI system currently uses it](https://searchengineland.com/google-says-normal-seo-works-for-ranking-in-ai-overviews-and-llms-txt-wont-be-used-459422). We publish one ourselves because it costs nothing, but we don't count on it.
- **Separate pages written for bots,** or pages stuffed with keywords.
- **Hundreds of AI-written pages** with nothing new in them.
- **Fake reviews and listings.** They break the review sites' rules and the trust you're trying to build.
- **Anyone who guarantees AI rankings.** Nobody controls what these assistants say.

## Where to start this week

Open Search Console and confirm your main pages are indexed. Rewrite the first paragraph of your most important page so it answers who, what, where, how long and how much. Then ask ChatGPT what it knows about your business, and write down the answer so you can compare it next month.
