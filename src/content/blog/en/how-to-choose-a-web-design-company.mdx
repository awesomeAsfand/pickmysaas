**The short answer:** choose the company whose answers you can check. Ask to see live websites they've built and talk to one of those clients, then compare quotes on what they actually include: who owns the domain and code, what you'll pay every year, who writes the Arabic, and what happens after launch. The twelve questions below cover all of it, in the order most buyers need them.

You don't need to know how websites are built to hire well. You need to ask the questions a good company is happy to answer and a weak one avoids.

## The 12 questions

### 1. Can I see live websites you've built, and talk to one of those clients?

Screenshots and design mock-ups prove someone can design. Live sites prove they can finish. Open two or three on your phone, and ask for a client you can message. A good company will also tell you which sites are client work and which are their own projects.

### 2. Who will own the domain, the code and the content?

You should own all three. The domain should be registered in your name with your own login. Ask whether you get the code if you leave, and whether any part of the site depends on a tool you'd lose access to.

### 3. Where will the site be hosted, and what will it cost me every year?

The build is a one-off; hosting, domain renewal and any licences are yearly. Ask for the yearly total in writing. If the company hosts the site for you, ask what happens to it if you stop paying them.

### 4. Is the price fixed, and what exactly does it include?

A fixed price with a written list of pages and features is easier to compare than an hourly estimate. Check what counts as extra: more pages, more design changes, content writing, translation, product uploads.

### 5. How long will it take, and what do you need from me?

Most delays come from waiting for content: texts, photos, prices, logins. A good company tells you when they need each thing from you, and whether the timeline counts from the day you pay or the day your content arrives.

### 6. Who writes and checks the Arabic, and is it truly right-to-left?

Ask whether the Arabic is written by a person or machine-translated, and who checks it. Then open an Arabic page they've built on your phone: menus, forms, icons and numbers should all read right to left, not just the paragraphs.

### 7. How fast are your websites on a phone?

Most Gulf customers browse on phones. Paste one of their live sites into Google's free [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/) and look at the mobile score. You don't need a perfect number, but a very slow portfolio site is a warning.

### 8. What SEO basics are included?

At minimum: a unique title and description for every page, a sitemap, connecting Google Search Console, sensible page addresses, and image descriptions. If you're replacing an old site, ask how they'll redirect the old addresses so you keep your Google traffic.

### 9. How will the site handle my customers' data?

Forms collect names, phone numbers and emails, so ask about a consent step, a privacy policy in Arabic and English, and where form messages are stored. Saudi Arabia and the UAE both have personal data protection laws; a good company knows the basics and tells you when to ask a lawyer.

### 10. How do I edit the site after launch, and what does support cost?

Ask what you can change yourself, what needs them, and what changes cost after launch. Is there a monthly care plan, and what does it cover: updates, backups, small edits, reports?

### 11. How do payments work?

Part upfront and the rest at launch, or at agreed milestones, is common and fair. Paying everything before work starts leaves you with no leverage. Ask for an invoice for each payment.

### 12. Where is your team, and how will we talk?

An office in your city is nice but not essential. What matters is how fast they reply, which hours they work, and whether you have one named person. Ask how you'll talk: WhatsApp, email, video calls, and how often you'll see progress.

## Red flags

- No live websites to show, only designs or templates
- The domain registered in the company's name, not yours
- No written list of what the price includes
- 100% payment before any work starts
- Vague answers about yearly costs
- Arabic that turns out to be machine translation nobody checked
- Promises to get you "number one on Google" by a date
