**The short answer:** a clinic website in the Gulf has to do two jobs. It must give patients what they look for before they book: who the doctors are, what each service involves, which insurance you take, where you are and how to book, in Arabic and English. And it must follow two sets of rules: health advertising rules, which usually need approval from your health regulator, and health data rules, which control where patient information can be stored.

Below is what to put on the site, then the rules to check before it goes live. We're not lawyers, and these rules change, so treat this as a checklist to take to your regulator, not legal advice.

## What patients look for

People choosing a clinic are often worried, in a hurry, or both. They want quick answers to the same few questions:

- **Who will I see?** Each doctor's name, specialty, qualifications, years of experience and the languages they speak
- **Can you treat my problem?** A clear page for each main service: what it is, who it's for, what happens at the visit
- **Do you take my insurance?** The insurance companies and plans you accept, kept up to date
- **Where are you, and when are you open?** The address on a map, parking, opening hours, and changed hours in Ramadan and on holidays
- **How do I book?** One tap to call or message on WhatsApp, or a short booking form
- **Can I trust you?** Your facility licence details, real photos of the clinic and team, and plain language without big promises

## The pages a clinic website needs

| Page | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| Home | What the clinic treats, where, and a booking button visible on every screen |
| Doctors | One profile per doctor: photo, specialty, qualifications, languages |
| Services | One page per main service, written for patients, not for other doctors |
| Insurance | The insurers and plans you accept |
| Location and hours | Map, parking, opening hours, Ramadan and holiday hours |
| Book an appointment | Call, WhatsApp and a short form |
| FAQ | The questions your reception team answers every day |
| Privacy policy | What the forms collect, why, and how long you keep it |

Write every page in Arabic and English, and design the Arabic properly right to left. Many patients in Saudi Arabia search in Arabic first, and in the UAE many search in English. A clinic that serves both should be found by both.

## Booking: keep it simple

Most Gulf patients already use WhatsApp, so a WhatsApp button with a message ready to send is often the easiest first step. Add a phone button for people who prefer to call, and a short form for those who don't want to message.

Keep the form short: name, phone number, preferred day and time, and which service or doctor. Don't ask for symptoms or medical history on the website; collect those at the clinic or in your clinic system. That makes booking faster and keeps sensitive data off your website (see the data rules below).

If you use online appointment software, check that it shows real availability and sends confirmations and reminders in the patient's language.

## Health advertising rules

**UAE.** Health advertising is regulated by the authority that licensed your facility: the Dubai Health Authority in Dubai, the Department of Health in Abu Dhabi, the Ministry of Health and Prevention in the other emirates, and its own regulator in Dubai Healthcare City. Advertising generally needs approval before it runs, and the rules commonly cover:

- showing your facility licence number
- avoiding absolute claims such as "the best" or guaranteed results
- before-and-after photos, which need the patient's consent and usually the regulator's approval
- posts by doctors and influencers that promote the clinic

**Saudi Arabia.** The Law of Private Health Institutions bans advertising health institutions except as its executive regulations and professional ethics allow, as the [Ministry of Health has reminded the sector](https://spa.gov.sa/en/N2261208). Advertising medicines and medical devices needs approval from the Saudi Food and Drug Authority, and prescription medicines can't be advertised to the public ([CMS guide](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/cms-expert-guide-to-advertising-of-medicines-and-medical-devices/saudi-arabia)).

**In practice, for your website copy:**

- describe services factually, and never promise results
- don't call the clinic or a doctor "the best" or "number one"
- show only real photos, with written consent from anyone identifiable
- keep prices and insurance information accurate
- ask your regulator whether your website counts as advertising, and keep its answer on file

## Patient data rules

**UAE.** Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 on technology in health fields generally requires health data about care given in the UAE to be stored inside the country, and allows transfers abroad only under set exceptions ([Taylor Wessing summary](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2021/08/me-health-data-in-the-uae-how-the-health-data-law-works-what-it-protects-and-what-exemptions-exist)). Most website hosting and many form tools store data abroad.

**Saudi Arabia.** The Personal Data Protection Law treats health data as sensitive data, which comes with stricter rules for collecting and sharing it ([DLA Piper overview](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?c=SA&t=law)).

**In practice:**

- keep website forms to booking details only: no symptoms, diagnoses or medical history
- add a consent step and link to a privacy policy in Arabic and English
- keep clinical records in your clinic system, hosted where the law requires, not in website form emails
- be careful what you discuss over WhatsApp; move clinical conversations to your clinic system

## Getting found

- **Google Business Profile.** A clinic has a real address, so claim your profile, keep the hours and phone number right, and answer reviews. For "clinic near me" searches it often matters more than the website.
- **One page per service, written in patients' words.** "Root canal treatment in Al Khobar" is what people type, not the clinical term alone.
- **Structured data.** Schema.org has a MedicalClinic type for your address, hours and phone number, which helps search engines read your details reliably.
- **Speed on phones.** Patients search on their phones, often while they're unwell or worried. A slow site loses them.
